# Cruising Life & Safety lesson research note

Step 2 of the Cruising Life & Safety module replaced six lesson skeletons with
finished teaching copy. This module makes more regulatory claims than any other
in the course, so the research behind it is recorded here in more detail than
the Boat Basics, Motoring, Navigation or Sails notes.

## The ASA chapter was available

ASA Chapter 3, *The Cruising Life*, pp. 46–63, was supplied as a nine-page scan
at `Crusing.pdf` outside the repository. As with the Boat Basics scan it has no
text layer, so it was read by rendering each page to an image; each scanned page
is a two-page spread, so the nine images cover pp. 46–63 exactly. It is not
committed to this repository and no part of it is reproduced here.

**How it was used**: as a curriculum and terminology source only — which topics
belong in this module, what the standard names are, and what order they are best
met in. Its diagrams were not reproduced or traced, its photographs were not
reused, and its page structure was not imitated.

On the question of prose, this note records what actually happened rather than
an intention. The **first draft** of lessons 1–4 did track the chapter too
closely: not wholesale copying, but sustained clause-level paraphrase in source
order that kept the chapter's distinctive turns of phrase and its choice of
illustrations. An independent verification pass read the rendered chapter pages
against the draft and caught it, listing roughly fifteen passages. Every one was
rewritten before release — restructured, re-exampled, and stripped of the
source's characteristic wording — and the affected passages were re-checked
against the chapter afterwards. Lessons 5 and 6 were unaffected: pp. 54–55 were
deferred to Motoring, and the chapter carries no fire, flooding, distress or
crew-injury material for lesson 6 to have drawn on.

A caveat belongs with that: fifteen is what one reader found on one pass, not
a proof that there were fifteen. A second pass over the rewrite found three more
isolated sentences of the same kind — the water-fill instructions, the stove
instruction sentence and the tether definition — which were then rewritten too.
Treat the count as evidence of a pattern that was corrected, not as an audited
total.

The lesson for the next module of this kind is that reading a source
page-by-page immediately before writing produces close paraphrase by default,
and that a separate reader comparing draft to source is the control that catches
it. A single review pass is not enough on its own.

Only part of the chapter is this module's. The mapping actually used:

| Chapter pages | Section | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| 46–47 | Chapter opener | Framing only |
| 48–49 | Safety Is Paramount; Safety Regulations | L1 |
| 50–51 | On-Deck Safety; Wearing Life Jackets | L2 |
| 52–53 | Additional Safety Equipment | L3 (recommended half) |
| 54–55 | Care and Feeding of an Auxiliary Engine | **Motoring's** — only the exhaust, engine-space and oil-discharge *hazard* angles were taken into L5; no operating or fuelling procedure |
| 56–59 | Living Quarters; Fresh Water; Garbage; Sanitation | L4, plus the LPG/alcohol stove and gimbal framing |
| 60–63 | Sail trim, balance, weather/lee helm, reefing | **Sails & Trim's** — deferred wholesale |

Nothing in the scanned chapter covers fire, flooding, MAYDAY procedure or crew
injury, so L6 is written from the accepted question bank plus the primary
sources below rather than from the chapter.

The chapter's own regulatory language was **not** carried over unchecked. Two
specific things in it are stale or incomplete against current rule:

- Its garbage panel reproduces the pre-2013 MARPOL Annex V discharge table
  (distance-banded discharge by garbage type). The current regime is
  prohibition-by-default with narrow exceptions. The lesson teaches the current
  framing and the placard requirement, not the old table.
- Its life-jacket material presents child wear as a state matter. There is a
  federal baseline as well (see the question correction below).

The accepted Practice question bank was the second authority: all 40 questions
mapped to the six lessons' concepts were read in full before any lesson copy was
written, so the copy and the bank agree.

## Primary sources checked

Every regulatory claim that survives into the finished copy was verified against
a primary source. Claims that could not be pinned to one were either dropped or
written as prudent practice rather than as law.

| Topic | Source | Result as taught |
|---|---|---|
| Wearable PFD carriage | 33 CFR 175.15(a) | One wearable PFD aboard for each person, used per its approval label. |
| Throwable device | 33 CFR 175.15(b) | Vessels 16 ft and over carry one throwable in addition to the wearables. |
| Child PFD wear | 33 CFR 175.15(c) | **Federal** rule: no one may operate a recreational vessel under way with a child under 13 aboard unless the child is wearing an approved PFD or is below decks / in an enclosed cabin. |
| Child PFD wear, state layer | 33 CFR 175.25 | Where a state has established its own child wear age, that state requirement applies on its waters *instead of* the federal one. Federal is a backstop, not a floor the states merely build on. |
| PFD labelling | 79 FR 56491 (2014 final rule, *PFD Labeling and Standards*, eff. 22 Oct 2014); 2024 *Lifejacket Approval Harmonization* final rule | Type I–V codes were removed from the carriage and labelling regulations; the rules speak of **wearable** and **throwable**. Devices already approved under the old labels remain approved and countable, and both label generations are in circulation. Copy uses wearable/throwable and names the Type codes only as legacy shorthand still seen on gear. |
| Visual distress signals — waters | 33 CFR 175.105 | Coastal waters = US waters of the Great Lakes, the territorial seas, and waters directly connected to them up to the first point where the shorelines narrow to 2 miles. |
| Visual distress signals — carriage | 33 CFR 175.110 | Boats 16 ft and over on those waters carry day-use and night-use devices (or combined). Boats under 16 ft need only night signals, and only between sunset and sunrise. A boat on an unconnected inland lake is outside the requirement's operating area entirely. |
| Sound-producing device | 33 CFR 83.33 (Rule 33) | Vessels under 12 m need not carry the prescribed whistle/bell but must have *some other means of making an efficient sound signal*. Purpose is the Rule 34/35 manoeuvring, warning and restricted-visibility signals. |
| Fire extinguisher carriage | 46 CFR 25.30-20; 33 CFR 175.320 (subpart D, 2022 relocation) | Carriage count by length; one 20-B substitutes for two 5-B. |
| Fire extinguisher ratings | *Fire Protection for Recreational Vessels* final rule, eff. 20 Apr 2022; USCG Boating Safety extinguisher FAQ | Model year 2018 and newer: 5-B or 20-B with a date stamp. Model years 1953–2017: those, **or** legacy B-I / B-II in good condition. A 10-B does not substitute for two 5-B. |
| Fire extinguisher age | Same | Disposable extinguishers must leave service 12 years after the date of manufacture stamped on the bottle. |
| Fire extinguisher condition | Same | "Good and serviceable": gauge in the operable band, pin seated, nozzle clear, no significant corrosion or damage. No logged inspection is required. |
| Marine sanitation devices | 33 CFR 159.7; 40 CFR 140.3; EPA vessel-sewage guidance | Type I and II treat and may discharge; Type I only on vessels 65 ft and under. Type III holds. Untreated sewage may not be discharged within three miles. |
| No-discharge zones | 40 CFR 140.4; EPA NDZ guidance; 33 CFR 159.7(b) | A state may bar all discharge, treated or not. In an NDZ the device must be physically secured — seacock closed and handle removed, padlocked, or wire-tied. |
| Garbage | 33 CFR 151.51, 151.59 | Plastics may not go overboard anywhere; all garbage discharge is prohibited in US navigable waters. US vessels 26 ft and over must display a garbage placard. |
| Oil discharge | 33 CFR 155.450; 40 CFR 110.3; 33 CFR 153.203 | Vessels 26 ft and over display a "Discharge of Oil Prohibited" placard. A discharge that causes a film, sheen or discoloration is reportable to the National Response Center. |
| VHF Channel 16 | 47 CFR 80.310; USCG NAVCEN | Recreational vessels are not generally required to carry VHF; a vessel that has one should keep a watch on 16 (or 9 for calling) when under way and not otherwise using the set. |
| MAYDAY structure | USCG NAVCEN emergency radio procedures | MAYDAY ×3; "this is" + vessel name ×3 and call sign; MAYDAY and name once more; position; nature of distress; assistance needed; number aboard and condition of any injured; then release and listen. |
| Carbon monoxide | CDC/NIOSH boating CO guidance; USCG Boating Safety CO pages | Sources are engines and generators; colourless and odourless; accumulates at slow speed, at idle, alongside and under a stern deck; symptoms overlap seasickness; marine-listed CO detectors and ventilation are the defence. |
| Shore power | ESFI and NFPA electric-shock-drowning guidance; ABYC E-11 (ELCI, 30 mA / 100 ms) | Framed as a hazard and a habit — listed cords in good condition, no swimming near an energised dock — not as a carriage requirement. |
| LPG stove installation | ABYC A-1 (via West Marine and Practical Sailor summaries) | Cylinder in a locker sealed from the interior and drained overboard; remote solenoid; shut off at the cylinder after cooking. Written as installation and habit, not as law. |
| Harness, tether, jacklines | US Sailing Safety Equipment Requirements; RYA safety-lines guidance; the ASA chapter's own safety tip | **Best practice, not law** for a recreational cruising boat. Clip inboard, to a jackline or a through-bolted padeye, never to the lifelines. |
| Three points of contact | The ASA chapter's own safety tip ("keep three points planted") | Taught as ASA guidance, not as a rule. |

Where a requirement varies by vessel length, operating area or state, the lesson
says so rather than stating a single number as universal. The federal/state and
required/recommended boundaries are kept explicit in every case: L3 is built as
two clearly separated halves, and the required half is a table with a column that
names the qualifying condition.

## Question factual audit

All 40 mapped questions were read in full against the sources above.
Thirty-nine are accurate as written. One carried a material factual error.

### Correction made

**`safety-pfd-child`** — its explanation stated that federal law sets the
baseline as carriage only ("one appropriately sized wearable PFD per person
aboard") and that "many states go further and require young children to actually
wear a PFD". That is wrong in the direction that matters: 33 CFR 175.15(c) is
itself a federal *wear* requirement for children under 13 on a recreational
vessel under way, and under 33 CFR 175.25 a state's own child wear age applies on
its waters *instead of* the federal rule rather than on top of it. A learner
taught the old wording would believe no federal wear rule exists.

The smallest correction that removes the false claim was made: the prompt no
longer frames the answer as "prudent practice", the correct choice now states the
federal under-13 rule and the state-supersession, the explanation and source were
rewritten to match, and two `whyWrong` strings were adjusted so they stay
consistent with the corrected answer. The other two distractors are untouched,
the correct choice id is unchanged (`a`), and no other question in the bank was
edited.

This rolls the concept-stripped question digest in `practice-concepts.test.ts`
for the third time in the project's history; the comment there records why.
`content.test.ts` gained a focused regression test pinning the corrected
question's federal citation, its under-13 threshold and the state-supersession
clause, so the old wording cannot come back unnoticed.

### Noted, not changed

Three things are dated or arguable but not wrong, and were left alone rather than
used as cover for editorial rewriting:

- `safety-pfd-type-id` and `safety-req-throwable-length` use the legacy "Type
  III" / "Type IV" codes. The codes are gone from the regulations but the
  devices they describe remain approved and countable, and the questions'
  substantive claims are correct. The lessons teach wearable/throwable and
  explain the legacy codes, which is where that reconciliation belongs.
- `safety-req-sound-device` has a "capable of a 4-second blast (or similar)"
  aside inside the `whyWrong` of an obviously-wrong "purely decorative" choice.
  Rule 33 imposes no such duration on a vessel under 12 m. It is hedged, it is
  not the answer, and it is not what the question tests.
- `emer-fire-classes-onboard` calls a cooking-oil fire Class B. That is correct
  in the USCG marine A/B/C scheme the question cites; NFPA 10 ashore separates
  cooking media as Class K, and no choice offers it.

## Figures

**No new asset was created**, so there is no Step 3 for this module. Six
already-approved manifest assets were reused:

| Lesson | Assets |
|---|---|
| L2 Staying On the Boat | `custom-pfd-wearable-throwable`, `custom-harness-tether-jackline` |
| L3 Safety Gear | `custom-visual-distress-flare`, `photo-fire-extinguisher-marine` |
| L6 Fire, Flooding & Calling for Help | `photo-fire-extinguisher-use`, `custom-flooding-seacock` |

L1, L4 and L5 carry no figure. Nothing in the manifest illustrates
responsibility aboard; `custom-cabin-layout` was considered for L4 and rejected,
because that lesson is about managing water, galley and head rather than finding
them, and the layout diagram is already Boat Basics' teaching; and
`custom-fueling-gas-diesel` was considered for L5 and rejected, because it is a
*fuelling-procedure* diagram and putting it here would drag Motoring's procedure
into a hazard lesson. Both would have been decoration.

The two extinguisher photographs were deliberately assigned to the lesson that
does *not* own them as a question asset, and each was opened and inspected: the
marine unit's rating label is not legible at any size, and the demonstration
photo shows only a person aiming at the base of a fire. Every figure used was
checked against its lesson's mapped questions and none exposes an answer.
`custom-flooding-seacock`'s visible labels describe the scenario
("BELOW-WATERLINE LEAK SCENARIO", "Water spraying in at loose clamp") and not the
response its mapped question asks for.

## Scope boundaries held

Verified from the content side and pinned by tests: this module does not reteach
Boat Basics' anatomy, cabin layout or system identification; Motoring's
engine-start, blower, fuelling procedure, controls or docking; Sails & Trim's
balance, trim, reefing or weather/lee helm; or Navigation's chart work, compass
or light recognition. Crew overboard, hypothermia, grounding, dragging anchor and
steering failure are Hands-On Cruising's and stay out; knots, line work, towing,
rigging failure and routine radio etiquette are Seamanship's and stay out. The
existing `practice-concepts.test.ts` guard already enforces the question side of
this; no mapping was broadened and no question was added.
